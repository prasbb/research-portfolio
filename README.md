# Research Portfolio — Pras Bairwa

I am an undergraduate Computer Science researcher at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), with interests in **information retrieval, machine learning, representation learning, and computational imaging**. My work explores how machine learning systems can identify meaningful structure in complex data and adapt to changing information needs. I conduct research at UIUC's Siebel School of Computing and Data Science and the Cellular Neuroscience Imaging Laboratory at the Beckman Institute.

This portfolio summarizes my current and recent research projects, including ongoing work and accepted presentations.

## Research Projects

### 1. Dynamic Alignment in Conversational RAG
**Adaptive information retrieval · User feedback**  
**UIUC Siebel School of Computing and Data Science** | Aug. 2026–present  
**Advisor:** Dr. Pablo Robles-Granda | **Senior thesis — ongoing**

Conventional retrieval augmented generation (RAG) systems often retrieve information without sufficiently accounting for differences in users' preferences and informational needs. At the same time, overreliance on historical feedback can cause a retrieval system to prioritize outdated preferences when the user's topic or intent changes. My senior thesis investigates a **persistent feedback state**, represented separately from the current query, that dynamically influences document rankings while preserving responsiveness to current intent. I am exploring semantic and attribute-based feedback, alongside LSTM and GRU based update mechanisms that learn when to retain or forget previous feedback.

**Status:** Ongoing research

### 2. Unsupervised Discovery of Cancer-Associated Morphologies
**Computational pathology · Unsupervised learning · Frequency-domain representations**  
**Cellular Neuroscience Imaging Laboratory, Beckman Institute, UIUC** | Ongoing  
**Research mentors:** Dr. Catherine Best-Popescu and Dr. Jorge Maldonado  
**IEEE BHI 2026 — accepted one-page abstract and poster (first author)**

This project studies whether quantitative image representations can reveal cancer-associated tissue patterns without requiring explicit labels for each morphology. Using malignant and benign breast tissue imagery, I combine **multiscale wavelet scattering**, **Fourier-domain descriptors**, and **unsupervised clustering** to characterize variation in tissue structure. 

**Research output:** *“Unsupervised Discovery of Cancer-Associated Morphologies Using Scattering and Frequency-Domain Representations”* - accepted for poster presentation at the **IEEE-EMBS International Conference on Biomedical and Health Informatics (BHI 2026)**, Hong Kong, December 12–15, 2026.
